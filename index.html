About

I am an undergraduate student in Chemistry at the University of California, Berkeley. My research interests lie at the intersection of chemistry, DNA origami, nanomaterials, molecular robotics, bioengineering, and computational design.

I am interested in using DNA as a programmable material to build nanoscale structures and systems with potential applications in self-assembly, biosensing, biomolecular design, and future molecular machines.

Research

DNA Origami and Programmable Nanomaterials

This project explores how DNA origami can be used as a programmable platform for constructing nanoscale structures. My work focuses on design principles, assembly behavior, and strategies for improving structural yield and scalability.

  • Designed and analyzed DNA-based nanostructures.
  • Studied self-assembly behavior and design constraints.
  • Connected molecular design with broader applications in nanomaterials and bioengineering.

Molecular Robotics and Self-Assembly

I am interested in molecular systems that can perform controlled nanoscale functions, including actuation, sensing, assembly, and information-guided behavior.

Computational Design of Molecular Systems

I am also interested in computational approaches for molecular design, including modeling, simulation, optimization, and potentially AI-guided design of DNA-based materials.
